This one is a difficult subject for me. I have gone to wildly different sides of the spectrum on this topic. At one point I thought rim brakes were basically useless. At one point I wanted to refit my touring bike with drum brakes.

I think I’ve settled on having a love/hate relationship with all of them. Yes, I am very much against new standards. Not only are there three different main types of brakes (disc, rim, and drum), but there are completely non-interchangeable subcategories within each type of brake.

For rim brakes, you have:
- centerpull and sidepull road brakes
- cantilever brakes
- v-brakes
- u-brakes
- whatever the hell rene herse is doing

For disc brakes:
- post mount and iso mount
- flat mount

And for drum brakes:
- sturmey archer style
- roller brakes

Seems like some bullshit. And to some extent, it is. Some of these offer no tangible benefit, like flat mount and U-Brakes, and are basically just ways to make bike frames incompatible with each other. However, most of these do have real trade offs.

Centerpull/sidepull brakes have the lowest tire clearence on this list, but require no braze-ons of any kind. This means they can be mounted on (in theory) 95% of bikes on the road today. In practice, however, I think they largely suck. As I said, low tire clearance. Also low braking power and they can make it extremely difficult to attach fenders. But still, there is something to be said about a universal standard that is also the easiest conceivable to manufacture.

Cantilever brakes introduced a new braze on (cringe), but allow for huge tires and huge fenders (based).

V-Brakes introduced a whole new cable pull (unbelievably cringe), have slightly less clearance than cantis (a bit cringe), but do brake better and are the easiest brakes to set-up on the market (based).

Disc brakes introduced yet more braze ons (cringe) changed a lot of wheel standards (infuriating and mega cringe) and refused to settle on a mount type or cable pull or even having a cable (cosmically cringe) but they offer significant improvements to braking power in the wet and the dirt, while also saving the wheel rim from needing to be a consumable part AND a structurally critical component at the same time (extremely based).

Drum brakes are heavy (who cares) but are also the most reliable and least needing of maintenance of any of the brakes on this list (so based that mere humans are not worthy). The hubs are sealed and brake just as well in the rain as they do in the not rain (PNW based) and they are not prone to letting out a deafening scream when they aren’t perfectly setup like most other brakes. Downsides are they don’t brake as fast as the other styles of brakes (PNW cringe) available and, while they are low maintenance, they need to be completely opened up if you do need to fix anything (nothing lasts forever…). Oh and they make your wheel a pain in the ass to take off.

Roller brakes are drum brakes that have a proprietary (criiiiiinge) mount that allows them to attach to the outside of certain shimano hubs. However, this makes them the only modular drum brakes on the market. This comes with the added benefits of being easier to maintain than classic drum brakes (holy based) and they have room for a better heat management (neat). Depending on the model, they supposedly have even less braking power than classic drum brakes (ouch).

If I had a magic wand and a spell that let me consolidate standards (please god, I would only use this power for good I swear) I could tell you which standard I would enforce for most bike parts. 27.2mm seatposts, schrader valves, etc. But for brakes I’m honestly at a loss. I *prefer* mechanical disc brakes disc brakes (BB7s, obviously), but that is because of where I live and the challenges my bikes face. People bike in all kinds of climates and terrains and with different budgets. There is no perfect brake.

There are bad brakes though. U-Brakes suck. Jan Heine needs to be arrested. And, as has been discussed at length, hydraulic brakes are for (mostly) for suckers.
