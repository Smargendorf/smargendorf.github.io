When I was 3, I was taken along on a family trip to Long Island. My parents were worried about me meeting Paul, my Great Grandpa. Paul was in a wheelchair, and they were worried I was going to make some comment on it and make Paul feel bad or just be scared of wheelchairs and not talk to him, I guess. Apperently (I barely remember the trip at all), when the door opened I ran up to Paul with my arms wide and yelled "Grandpa!" My parents looked at eat other and sighed.

I was told this story when I was in highschool. At the time, I remember thinking how relieved my parents must have been that their kid wasnt a little shithead who made fun of disabled old people (yay me, I guess). I was down on myself at the time, and my mom was telling me the story to prove to me that deep down I had always been a kind person. But, looking back, I wonder if their real worry was embarrasing themselves in front of Paul.

Paul Townsend is the man who I get my last name from, but not in the usual way american last names are passed down. My mom's maiden name was Baker, and my dad's "maiden" (bachelor?) name was Moored. My parents hated their last names growing up. My father had long since been estranged from his own father, and while my mother loved her father, she had no connection to his family and apperently just didnt like how Baker sounded (??).

So, when they got married, they *BOTH* changed their last names to my mother's mother's last name: Townsend. Wendy Townsend, daughter of Paul, never changed her last name to Baker. My parents liked it the best of all the "available" family names because of the history and class that came with it. So, I'm a Townsend. In fact, me and my brother are the only decendants of Paul in our generation that share his last name, just for a kind of strange reason.

Anyway, back to my parents witnessing their 3 year old son not bully an old guy in a wheelchair. Paul was a big deal in Long Island. I wasn't just running with my arms opened wide into some random house in the suburbs. This was a HUGE waterfront property along the north coast of Long Island. The living room was filled with awards and framed newspaper clippings. The mudroom had a water feature surrounded with beautiful japanese art of dubious origin. My parents were in the middle of a shaky marriage, were pregnant with their second child before they were even 30, hadn't finished school yet, and had taken his last name despite tradition. Paul was someone my parents didn't want to be embarrassed in front of.

---

The Townsends have a lot of history in Long Island. There is even a museum to the Townsends on Long Island who played a fairly large part in the revolutionary war (we caught Benedict Arnold and I've never let my friend who went to West Point forget it).
